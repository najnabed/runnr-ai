The blind spot

Every runner has a form.
Almost none of us have seen ours.

You can feel effort, pace and pain. The mechanics producing all three happen behind you, below you, and roughly 170 times a minute.

01

Pain arrives late

A niggle in the shin or knee is the symptom of something your stride was doing for weeks beforehand. By the time it hurts, the habit is well rehearsed.

02

Mirrors are moments

A gym mirror or a race photo gives you one frame out of thousands — usually the frame where you were trying to look like a runner.

03

Coaching doesn't scale

A gait analysis means a lab, an appointment and a fee. Useful once. Useless on a Tuesday evening when the habit actually shows up.

The reframe

Good form was never talent. It's feedback you've never had.

Coaching has always come down to a trained pair of eyes and one sentence at the right moment. The eyes are the expensive part — and you have been carrying a very good pair in your pocket the entire time. Runnr AI simply points them at you.

How it works

Prop it up. Run. Adjust while it still matters.

No pods, no straps, no foot sensors, no subscription hardware. Your camera and its AI do the analysis.

1

Set your phone down

Lean it against the treadmill console or set it beside your drill space, side-on or head-on. Framing guidance appears on screen.

2

Start running

Runnr AI locks onto your posture and body alignment and tracks joint positions continuously as you move — no calibration ritual.

3

Correct in real time

Cues surface as you run, not in a report three days later. Then review the session analytics to see whether the change actually held.

Real-time posture tracking

Trunk position, alignment and joint relationships read live while you're moving, not reconstructed afterwards.

Body alignment analysis

Where your segments sit relative to each other and to the vertical — the asymmetries you cannot feel from the inside.

Run analytics

Session data that connects how you moved to what you produced, so form stops being an abstraction and becomes a trend.

Camera-native

Uses the camera already in your device. Nothing to charge, clip on, thread through a lace or replace next season.

Treadmill or drills

Built for the two places form work actually happens: a fixed-position run, or deliberate stride practice.

Across your devices

iPhone and iPad, plus Apple silicon Mac and Apple Vision. 2.3 MB — smaller than the photo you took at the finish line.

The idea Runnr AI is built on

About Us

Built by two runners who got tired of guessing.

Runnr AI started as a domestic argument about technique — the kind that can only be settled by evidence nobody had. We could measure distance, pace, heart rate and elevation. We could not answer a simpler question: what does my body actually do when I run?

The tools that could answer it lived in clinics and labs. The device that could answer it was sitting on the kitchen counter. So we built the coach we wanted — something that watches carefully, says one useful thing at the moment it's useful, and then gets out of the way.

We are a small independent team, not a fitness corporation. That shows up in the product: no engagement streaks, no leaderboard, no pressure to run today. Just a clear look at your form, free, whenever you want it.

What's the Vision

Movement coaching should be as ordinary as a step count.

Form feedback is currently a privilege — reserved for athletes with coaches and patients with referrals. It shouldn't be. Here is where we're taking it.

Horizon 01

A coach in every room where someone runs

Get the core loop right for as many people as possible: point a camera, get an honest read on your posture, understand the one change worth making today. Free, fast, and light enough to live on any device you already own.

Horizon 02

From single sessions to a movement history

One run is a snapshot; the value is in the drift. We want to show how your mechanics change across weeks — as fatigue accumulates, as shoes wear down, as a training block deepens — so a pattern is visible while it's still a pattern and not yet an injury.

Horizon 03

Beyond running

Gait is the first case, not the only one. The same camera-and-alignment approach applies to walking after an injury, to a lifting pattern, to anyone rebuilding confidence in how they move. Ambitious, and deliberately so.

A note on honesty: Horizon 01 is what ships today. Horizons 02 and 03 are where we're heading, not features you can open tonight. We'd rather tell you which is which.
